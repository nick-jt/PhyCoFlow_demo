## 0. The one decision that shapes everything

The paper currently sells four claims. After today's audit, **claim 2 is not defensible as written**, and
we have the material for a better version of it.

| # | Claim | Status | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scaling separation** — constant memory 64³→1024³ vs OOM at 320³/640³ | **SAFE** | correct the numbers only |
| 2 | **Accuracy + best calibration**, "decisive" at t≈9–28 | **NOT DEFENSIBLE** | **restructure** (below) |
| 3 | **Deployed sensing** — worst case beats every baseline's best | **SAFE** | fix the protocol sentence |
| 4 | **Native geometry** — volumetric from surface-only | **SAFE BUT THIN** | needs ≥1 baseline |
| 5 | *(new)* **When is a generative reconstructor calibrated?** | **AVAILABLE** | **promote to a contribution** |

**Why claim 2 fails:** the metric's sensitivity to the operating point exceeds the advantage claimed over
the baseline.

- NFE 4→16 moves spread/error **+0.217**, coverage **+0.127**
- K 8→32 moves coverage **+0.107**
- Claimed advantage over latent FM: **+0.097 / +0.087**

Plus calibration drifts with training on all three datasets (JHU −0.275, wing −0.090, FireBench −0.019),
and `best.pt` selection systematically picks the higher-calibration checkpoint.

**The replacement claim, which the evidence supports:** predictive spread is a **fixed function of
distance-to-nearest-sensor**, identical at every density, with a floor that never falls. Density changes
only the *mixture* of near/far points, which is what sweeps spread/error from 0.44 to 1.12 and crosses
ideal near 10% coverage. Nobody has reported this. It subsumes the unobserved-channel failure (flat in
sensor count, worse than a constant predictor) as the same phenomenon at infinite sensor distance.

## Standing rules (earned today — each cost us something)

1. **Measure the second case before broadcasting the first.** Four retractions today came from generalising one measurement.
2. **Read the output, not the exit code.** The `--nf-accum` violation, four CoNFiLD smoke cycles, and two fabricated duty cycles were all visible in output and invisible in exit status.
3. **A submitted job is not an armed job.** The S3GM watchdog failed in 6 s and left training unguarded; `DependencyNeverSatisfied` does not catch it.
4. **Never compare relL2 across normalisation schemes.** ±1 vs z-scored made a codec bound look 23% better.
5. **Every calibration number carries its operating point** (density, NFE, K, checkpoint rule) or it means nothing.
6. **Visual inspection outranks aggregates.** A figure corrected a claim I had already written down, within an hour of figures being requested.
7. **Retire run dirs with a PREFIX** (`ABORTED_*`), never a suffix — a suffix leaves them matching the glob.
7b. **Never collapse z when plotting a 3-D field.** Slice at fixed z; never `.mean(axis=z)`, never
   triangulate all N points on (x,y). This bug has appeared **three times**, is invisible in every
   metric, and makes every method look better and more similar than it is. Run
   `python src/check_no_zcollapse.py` before any figure goes in the paper.
8. **Generous-to-the-baseline is defensible; stingy is not.** Every unfairness found today ran in our favour.
